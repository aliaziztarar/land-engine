# Appraisal

One clear set of numbers that the user's programme reviews before any figure goes to an owner, agent or promoter. Every input has a source and a date. Nothing is estimated in prose; it is computed in the workbook or the template. Explain each term to the user the first time.

Start from the site note. If there is no site review yet, run the site-review skill first (or at least its steps 1 to 6), because the appraisal rests on the council's rules and the scheme range found there. Read `profile/profile.md` for the user's programme rules (target profit, who reviews, deal structure rules) and `profile/profile.json` for their workbook and calculator files.

## The structure
1. **Gross development value (GDV)**: homes by type and size, each priced per square foot from local sold comparables (section A); affordable homes at the registered provider's price (section C).
2. **Build and development costs**: build rate (section E), utilities, externals, contingency, professional fees, warranty, building regulations, sales costs, finance.
3. **Planning obligations**: affordable housing (in GDV), CIL, S106, nutrient or water neutrality, biodiversity net gain if not in costs, Building Safety Levy on schemes of 10 or more homes (affordable homes exempt; rate by area from the regulations).
4. **Builder's profit**: the programme's target profit on sale (`profile/profile.json`, `target_profit_on_sale`; BuildUp's rule from 17 November 2026 is 26 percent). Never lower it to make a site work; if a site only works at a lower profit, it does not work.
5. **Consented land value**: GDV less items 2 to 4. What a builder could pay for the site with permission.
6. **Less overage or uplift owed to a third party** (a previous seller's clause, a promoter's share), measured as the clause says. Read the deed; never assume.
7. **Less planning costs**: consultant, architect and masterplan, flood and drainage, transport, ecology and nutrient budget, trees, archaeology, landscape, heritage, application and pre-application fees, legal, and an appeal contingency for a speculative application. A village edge scheme of 12 to 20 homes typically runs to about £90,000 to £130,000; larger sites up to about £300,000. A workbook default may be a percentage of GDV; state which was used.
8. **The remainder** is shared between the landowner and the user (and their programme, if it is a joint venture). The split is negotiated and goes to the programme first. Buying costs (legal, stamp duty, any introduction fee) come off before the margin is real.

**Quick test**: if the scheme does not reach the target profit with the land at £0, it is not viable; stop and say so plainly.
**Offer range**: the floor is the owner's current value (what they paid, or field value with a margin above it); the ceiling is the consented land value less overage, planning costs and a fair remainder for the user. One set of numbers; a sensitivity is a short second table, not a second appraisal. State the break even price per square foot.

## A. Comparables (sold, local, similar)
- At least five good comparables per house type (BuildUp's requirement). Start within a quarter of a mile and go out to one mile; sold in the last one to two years; non listed homes of similar type, size, age and quality. New build is not required. Leave out listed buildings and small cottages (small homes inflate the price per square foot).
- Price per square foot = sold price (Rightmove or Zoopla sold prices, which show price and date) divided by the EPC floor area (find-energy-certificate.service.gov.uk by postcode; multiply square metres by 10.764). EPCs can predate extensions and exclude garages; identical floor areas on one street are a warning to re-check.
- Check bedrooms and sale history on the sold record, not the agent's listing; an asking price that has not sold is not evidence.
- Choose by similarity, never by price; taking only the high sales is cherry picking. Price per square foot falls as homes get bigger; price each type on its own evidence. A new build premium over modern second hand homes is usually 10 to 20 percent (a general pattern; say so if used).
- Thin local evidence: widen in steps (two miles, three miles, the nearest town) and say so; a named local agent's view with examples; the programme's own pricing view.
- Every comparable has a link the user can open: address, type, size, build year, EPC band, price, date, price per square foot, link. Detail pages often block the fetch tool; use the browser, or ask the user to paste links.

## B. Section 106
- Three approved residential applications of 10 or more homes in the same council area in the last six months (BuildUp's requirement); widen to twelve months if there are not three, and say so. Search as in `.claude/skills/searchland/SKILL.md` (planning searches). Also use the precedent file from the site review, which already holds S106 items for nearby schemes.
- For each: the signed S106 agreement or unilateral undertaking (amounts), the decision notice (permitted "subject to" S106), the officer or committee report (Heads of Terms). Record only cash per home (education by phase, health, open space and play, highways, libraries, waste, habitats payments, monitoring); on site delivery is not cash. A committee resolution or a deferred item is not an approval. Check the application is in the right council.
- Where the local school or surgery is full, use the highest well evidenced figure and say why. Councils generally cannot seek cash on schemes of 10 homes or fewer.

## C. Affordable housing (from the site review, step 6)
The policy percentage, threshold and tenure split, valued at what a registered provider pays: social rent typically about 40 to 50 percent of open market value, affordable rent about 50 to 60 percent, shared ownership about 65 to 70 percent, First Homes at the policy discount. Use the programme's figures or a local registered provider's where known, and say which. Registered providers usually prefer houses to flats and two bedroom homes to one.

## D. CIL and nutrient or water neutrality
- CIL: the council's charging schedule (rate per square metre, zone, date, exemptions), or the council's own statement that it has none. Never from memory.
- Neutrality: whether the site is in a catchment (SearchLand layer, Natural England maps); the mitigation the council accepts (its own credit scheme, private credits, on site measures); the nutrient budget per home from recent comparable applications' nutrient assessments, not a textbook figure; the credit price only from an official page or a signed purchase agreement in an application, with the date. If no official price exists, say so and stop on that line. It is a cost and a delay, not a block.

## E. Build cost
- From the programme's costing calculator (`profile/profile.json`, `buildup_calculator`). For BuildUp's calculator: Calculator tab; Area (Broad Area, County, Location, the district as the cost data names it); Build type (New build, Estate housing, 2-storey for houses; Flats where the scheme is flats); Specification Medium; Date start the quarter building would begin (for a site at the start of planning, about two years ahead). Result B is the rate per square foot; Result A per square metre. How it works, so it can be explained: Medium is the mean of the cost data for that location (Low the lower quartile, High the upper); the start quarter applies the cost index movement since the data's base quarter (the "factor"). Record the selections and the result with the figure.
- If the calculator is not in the profile, use the copy supplied with the engine, `templates/buildup-costing-calculator.xlsx` (BuildUp's Costing Calculator 4, copied November 2026): copy it to `profile/buildup/` and record it in `profile/profile.json` as `buildup_calculator`. Ask the member only if BuildUp has issued a newer version.
- The calculator rate is the houses only; utilities, externals, contingency and fees are separate lines.

## F. The best mix (the lead's job)
Within the council's rules (affordable percentage and tenure, mix policy, space standards, density) and the site's net developable area, test several schemes and choose the one that gives the highest consented land value while still reading as a scheme the council has approved nearby (the precedent profile):
- vary the number of homes across the site review's range;
- vary the open market types (semis, detached, bungalows) and sizes where local comparables support the price;
- set the affordable homes to the tenure split and the types registered providers want;
- check density against what nearby approved schemes achieved.
Show the tested options in a short table (homes, mix, GDV, land value) and say why the chosen one wins. Explain the density reasoning when the home count is below the simple arithmetic: net area after flood edges, access, open space, drainage ponds, on site biodiversity and a landscape buffer; village edge schemes commonly net 20 to 25 homes per hectare.

## G. The programme's workbook and submission
- If the programme has a workbook (`profile/profile.json`, `buildup_workbook`), copy it for the site as `<TITLE> - appraisal.xlsx` in the site's folder, clear the previous inputs, keep every formula. `templates/README.md` explains the cells of BuildUp's Master Basic Appraisal. The Site Value cell holds the landowner's price only, never the residual; the target profit cell holds the programme's target. Add the joint margin rows described in the README. Read values back after writing them to check the workbook calculated.
- Otherwise use `templates/appraisal-template.md`.
- **BuildUp Nexus** (nexus.justbuildup.com), the "submit a request" form. Its fields, as the user pasted them on 19 November 2026, and what the engine prepares for each (all marked * are required; an incomplete submission is closed without review):
  1. Title Number: the main title.
  2. Link to the Appraisal Document or Google Drive folder, set to "anyone with the link can view". The user makes the link (upload the workbook and the pack to Google Drive, Share, "Anyone with the link"); the engine says exactly which files go in the folder.
  3. Accommodation Schedule: the engine writes it (homes by type, bedrooms, floor area, affordable or market, and which are drawings, sketches or standard allowances, with a note of the assumptions where standard allowances are used).
  4. Comparable data: at least 5 good comparables for EACH house type (including affordable types). The engine checks the count per type and refuses to say "yes" if any type has fewer than 5 (use fewer house types in the mix rather than thin evidence).
  5. CIL rate researched and correct: the engine confirms it from the council's current page and says the zone and year.
  6. At least 3 approved applications for residential development in the local authority area in the last 6 months for the S106 average: the engine lists them (reference, homes, decision date, S106 cash per home).
  7. Appraisal sheet completed in full: the engine reads back every input cell and checks none is blank or still holds the previous site's value.
  8. Deal structure: free text in the first person. The user's process: BuildUp reviews the site and either gives the go-ahead on the figures or asks for amendments; the user then negotiates with the owner on those figures, agrees the option or conditional agreement with the owner, and hands the site to BuildUp to take forward. The user pays for no surveys or reports in that process: the programme carries out the analysis. Write it in that order, in line with `profile/profile.md` (no interest, no conventional option fee), and ask for feedback on the figures.
  9. Other information: free text in the first person: the planning route and the policy position, what supports the site (see "How the submission documents are written" below), the owner's position and how the owner came to the user. Facts and evidence only; the reviewers make the assessment.
  10. Supporting documents (site plans, title plans, drawings): the title register and plan if bought; the site plan or map; any sketch.
  11. An aerial shot of the site from SearchLand (the engine makes it with the Screenshot map button, see the searchland skill, or asks the user for it).
  12. Site Checks and Due Diligence checklist: BuildUp's checklist (supplied with the engine) completed, as a PDF (section H below).
  13. Two tick boxes (everything submitted in full, including 5 comparables per house type; the Google Drive link is open to anyone with it). Only the user ticks these; the engine tells them in one line whether each is true.
  14. Proof the landowner is open to selling (upload): the owner's own message, email or text. A call note is not proof; the engine asks the user for a screenshot of the text or the email.
  Write both text boxes in the first person ("I"), following "How the submission documents are written" below. The uploaded checklist and the text must match. The engine writes every answer into one file, `<TITLE> - Nexus submission.md`, in the site's folder, with a tick list at the end that says for each box whether it is ready or what is missing.

## H. BuildUp's Site Checks and Due Diligence checklist (always filled and sent with the Nexus submission)
The checklist comes with the engine: `templates/buildup-site-checks-checklist.docx`, BuildUp's own Site Checks and Due Diligence checklist (copied 19 November 2026; BuildUp's Google Doc of it is https://docs.google.com/document/d/1tLo4lPR_rUHddV_f_MJHUhCYjCcT1ifHKeugpz7IWVo/edit?usp=sharing ). Use it as it is; nothing is needed from the member. If BuildUp issues a newer version, the member can download it as a Word file and say where it is: copy it to `profile/buildup/site-checks-checklist.docx`, which the fill tool then uses in place of the one supplied.
When: the checklist itself says "Please ensure you have conducted 1st and 2nd site checks before submitting your appraisal", so it is filled at the end of the appraisal, after the site review and before the Nexus submission; it never waits for BuildUp.
Its lines, so the answers can be prepared from the site note:
- **1st site checks** (rule out non starters): Access (adequate access); Sustainability (supporting road, connecting footpath); Flood Zone; Conservation Area; Heritage Assets; Radon Gas; Article 4; Green Belt; Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty (now called National Landscape); Sites of Special Scientific Interest; Tree Preservation Order; Nutrient Neutrality; Planning History.
- **2nd site checks** (only if no non starter in the first): Bio Diversity; Local Plan Policy.
- **3rd site checks** (only after a positive response from BuildUp): Contamination; Unexploded Bombs; Caves; Archeology.
- **4th site checks**: Title Register. Additional notes.
How to fill it:
1. Answer every line from the site note and the coverage checklist, one or two plain sentences each, each with the source or "SearchLand" and the date, following "How the submission documents are written" below. For a line not yet researched, write what is known and "not yet checked" (and, where it applies, "a standard report at the planning stage"); never leave a line blank and never write a guess as a fact. A first check that is a non starter (a hard constraint) stops the submission: tell the user in chat.
2. Write the answers into a small JSON file in the site's folder (one key per line, for example "Access", "Flood Zone", "Green Belt", "Local Plan Policy", "Title Register", "Additional notes", "Title number"), then run `python tools/fill_site_checks.py <answers.json> "<site folder>" "<TITLE> - Site Checks"`. The tool stops and lists any line without an answer, and writes the Word file and a PDF. Run it without -I (it imports python-docx).
3. Read the PDF back as text and check every line carries its answer and nothing is cut off. Upload the PDF in the Nexus "Site Checks and Due Diligence" box.
4. Record in the site note that the checklist was completed and on what date. If the site note changes before submission, refill it, so the checklist and the Nexus text always match.

## How the submission documents are written (Site Checks, Nexus answers, any note that goes to the programme)
The programme's reviewers are experienced and make the assessment of every site. A submission's job is to give them everything they need to do that well: complete facts and evidence, each with its source and date, set out plainly. The engine does not add its own opinion of the site to these documents, because the reviewers' judgement is the one that counts; the engine's own view is given to the user in full in the site note and in chat. These rules apply only to the documents sent to the programme. With the user, the engine is always completely frank: every risk, weak point and number that does not work is said plainly.
- Give the facts and the evidence the form asks for, each with its source and date. The comparables go in the workbook's comparables tab as found, with their sources. State the value used and the evidence it rests on. Constraints, planning history and policy are set out as found.
- Add no commentary, ratings or labels ("the main risk", "a strong site", "falls short", a median set against the value used). The reviewers draw their own conclusions from the evidence.
- Where a check has not been done yet, say "not yet checked" and, where it applies, "a standard report at the planning stage". Never leave a line blank and never write a guess as a fact.
- Describe the user's role accurately: they find the site, agree terms with the owner once the programme has reviewed it, and hand the site over. The programme runs the planning, surveys and reports, so the documents do not say the user will commission or pay for them.
- Answer everything the form asks for truthfully and completely (title, comparable counts per house type, CIL zone and rate, the approved applications and their S106).
- Write it in the first person, courteously, as you would to a mentor.

## Rules
- Never show a landowner an appraisal; they receive an offer after the programme's review. Never tell anyone what percentage of value an owner "should" get.
- Follow the deal structure rules in `profile/profile.md` (for example faith based finance rules: no interest, no conventional option fee).
- Date every price and rate. Name the three inputs that would move the answer most, and what would confirm each.
- If the first run is not viable at local values, say so plainly, with what would have to change for it to work.

## Finish
Write the inputs with sources to `knowledge/sites/<TITLE>-appraisal-inputs.md`, update the site note, and offer the next step in one line: "Would you like me to prepare the BuildUp Nexus submission now?" or, after the programme's review, "Shall I write to the owner?"

## Learned
- SearchLand's title report is a useful inputs pack (constraints, plan status, supply, affordable policy, CIL, nearby sales); it is not an appraisal.
- Committee report packs on a council's democracy site are readable when the planning portal blocks reading.
- BuildUp's Master workbook (Google Sheets export): rows 106 and below on the Base tab, and the notes cells Q38, Q53, Q67 and Q74, sit inside merged ranges: write the joint margin rows from row 108 and write notes only to the top left cell of each range. Delete the "Alt Mix" tabs of the previous site. Replace the label cells that hold FILTER formulas with plain text. Keep the Site Value cell for the owner's price only (0 until a price exists), then read the residual from the Residual Land Value cell.
- The costing calculator may list a county under an unexpected region in its dropdown file; LibreOffice cannot run its dropdown check. Reproduce Result B from the Prices Data mean and the index factor, and say so.
- A free council report may give the S106 of the neighbouring scheme in full; committee report papers are often readable on the council\'s democracy site (mgconvert2pdf links) when the planning portal refuses the fetch tool. Rightmove sold pages (house-prices/<postcode>.html) load with plain fetch and show price, date, type and bedrooms; EPC floor areas come from find-energy-certificate.service.gov.uk by postcode. HM Land Registry price paid data carries a new build flag.
- If the evidence only run fails the target profit at nil land, say so plainly and show the sensitivities (new build premium, affordable percentage, number of homes) in a short table; do not lower the target.
- BuildUp's Master workbook, third use (3 home scheme, 18 November 2026): the fee total on the Base tab adds rows 65 to 70 only, so adoption fees, bonds and the developer legal fee (rows 71 to 73) are never in any total; set rows 71 and 72 to nil for private drives and note it. Unmerge the banner cells in the Comparables evidence area (rows 39 down) before writing there. Edit with openpyxl, then check by converting a copy with LibreOffice (soffice --headless --convert-to xlsx) and reading the values back with data_only; a file saved by openpyxl has no stored values until recalculated. Write the CIL rate into the Fact Find tab (B3) and the existing floor area (B2), not into the Base cell. Keep a small model of the arithmetic to run the options and sensitivities, and check it against the workbook on the base.
- On a minor scheme (under 10 homes) CIL, biodiversity net gain, any habitats payment and the planning budget are the large items; S106 cash is usually nil. CIL zones are often by ward in a table in the charging schedule PDF (read page images if the text does not extract, and use the council's current rate page for the year of the likely permission).
- Affordable housing on small schemes (November 2026): some councils apply the national rule that affordable housing is sought on any site of 0.5 ha or more even with fewer than 5 homes, and takes a cash sum when providers would not buy so few homes; the figure comes from a viability review and can be found in the Housing Officer's consultation response and the officer report. For a pending application, read the officer report, Housing response, viability report and CIL form on the council register first: they give the real obligations, the mix and sizes, and the applicant's own cost and value assumptions to compare with the engine's.
- Existing buildings reduce CIL: the CIL form lists existing floorspace; buildings in lawful use for 6 of the last 36 months are netted off, and the council decides. Run the CIL both ways.
- Workbook method, fourth use (November 2026): copy the most recent filled workbook in the letters folder (it already has the joint margin rows and a Versions tab) rather than the blank Master. Base!G8 and G9 (affordable unit counts) are typed, not linked to the Comparables tab, so set them or the affordable homes vanish from the GDV. Notes in Q38 and Q53 only (the Q38:S52, Q53:S66, Q67:S73 and Q74:S82 ranges are merged; writing to any other cell in them fails). Contingency and fees apply to the rows 51 to 61 build and utilities only, not to the abnormals in rows 41 to 49: keep the engine's model the same so the two agree to the pound. Comparables tab: lay out one block per house type (title, header, spec row linked to the accommodation row, then 7 or 8 comparables); the affordable types need their own comparables too. The Cashflow, S Curve and NormalDist tabs throw errors in LibreOffice for every copy; they do not feed the Base result.
- In some cities S106 cash is effectively nil (education and health paid through CIL, no open space money on smaller sites), so the three-approvals evidence is mostly the council's own wording plus schemes that are 100 percent affordable; list the approvals and what each one's S106 secured, factually. Affordable housing can differ between the adopted guide, a windfall Green Belt site and an allocation in an emerging plan; read each.
- Evidence-only prices will often fail the 26 percent target in a lower value area. Show the break even new build premium (here 15.3 percent) and the asking-price cross checks, and never raise the base price to make a site work.
- Write the Nexus answers into one file per site (`<TITLE> - Nexus submission.md`) with a ready or not ready tick list; the user makes the Google Drive link, supplies the owner's own message as proof, and ticks the two boxes.
- Stay on BuildUp's template (19 November 2026, user's correction): planning costs are the 1.5 percent provisional sum of GDV, utilities are 7,500 pounds a home, build rate from the calculator. Never add the engine's own allowances (roads, junctions, adoption, footways) without evidence; if the council's own evidence says a cost is missing (for example external works at 5 to 15 percent of build cost), tell the user and show the effect as a sensitivity, and let them decide. An allowance needs a source (for example a cost set by a planning condition) or it stays out.
- Price evidence for a new build scheme: the council's own viability assessment (examination library, HDH Whole Plan Viability Assessment and its Further Note) gives the value per sq m, the registered provider percentages of market value, benchmark land values, S106 and externals assumptions; use it as the tested value, cross checked against sold new build prices (HM Land Registry SPARQL with the new build flag, matched to EPC floor areas by postcode and address) and the UK House Price Index (landregistry.data.gov.uk/data/ukhpi/region/<council>/month/<yyyy-mm>.json) to index a 2022 value. Never use one premium developer's prices alone, and say whether prices are before incentives (unknown).
- If a workbook is open in Excel the file is locked and cannot be overwritten: write a new version name, then swap the names once the user has closed Excel.

- Submission wording (November 2026): the programme reviews and analyses each site; the user then agrees terms with the owner on the reviewed figures, signs the option and hands the site over. The programme runs the planning and every survey and report. See "How the submission documents are written".
