# Rules for letters and approaches (binding)

These rules come from what has worked with real landowners and from BuildUp's teaching. The user's own additions, if any, are in `profile/profile.md` and take precedence. In the examples, `{name}`, `{short_name}`, `{role}`, `{company}`, `{mobile}`, `{email}`, `{company_description}` and `{county}` come from `profile/profile.json`.

## How the business is described
- A land and development company: "{company}, {company_description}". Never "small company". The signature title is the user's role from the profile, never "Founder" unless that is their role.
- When the model needs describing (second letter, call, email), use the user's business model from `profile/profile.md`. For a typical option or conditional contract model: "We take a site through planning at our own cost and risk, and the land stays in your name until it's sold with permission in place. You see the plans before they go anywhere. If planning isn't granted, you keep your land and owe nothing." A contribution to the owner's own solicitor's fees, if the user offers one, is a trust signal worth saying.
- Never describe how the land is sold on after planning, or name partners or funders; any arrangement with a programme or funder is private.
- Never teach an owner negotiating levers (exclusivity, minimum price, overage, end dates) or encourage them to compare offers. Never state figures that anchor against the user (percentages of value an owner "should" get).
- Never claim a track record the user does not have. Never inflate the team.

## Tone and structure (first letter)
- One page, short paragraphs, the key reassurance lines on their own. Plain, flowing English; no punchy or poetic lines; no em or en dashes; no exclamation marks.
- Never announce what a paragraph is doing ("the reason I'm writing now is"); just say it. No headings.
- Never suggest others may have written to them. Never "I'd guess I'm not the first".
- Do not explain the business model, the sale or any jargon in a first letter; say the planning cost is covered and invite a conversation.
- No desperation and no deadline ("hoping to settle on a site by the end of the year" is rejected). The "active need" line alone carries the why now.
- Never "buy". Never the land's planning status, the council's view, a number of homes, or that the land was put forward for a number of homes.
- No "I've enclosed a map" line; the map speaks for itself.
- Where there is history, show you have done the homework quietly (an appeal that went their way) without announcing it.
- Paraphrase BuildUp's template points in your own words and not in its order; other members send near identical letters.

## Approved wording
- Opener: "Please excuse the direct approach." (or "I hope you'll forgive the direct approach.")
- Who: "My name is {short_name} and I'm {role} at {company}, {company_description}."
- Interest, early and specific to their land: "We've looked at it closely, and it's a site we'd very much like to take on." and "We have an active need for land in this part of {county}, and your <field/land> meets what we're looking for."
- Questions and reassurance together: "I understand this comes with a lot of questions, and you may well have some hesitations. But rest assured, it's considerably simpler than most people expect, and most owners we speak to are surprised at what can be offered and how the process works."
- Planning cost, on its own line: "We pay for the planning work ourselves."
- Conversation: "If you're curious, I'd welcome a conversation. It would be informal, with no obligation on either side, and you'd come away knowing exactly what the options are, so that if you ever wanted to take things further, now or in the future, you could decide with all the facts in hand. If the timing isn't right, I'd be grateful if you'd keep this letter."
- Close: "Please reach out to me directly on {mobile}, or by email at {email}." then "Thank you for reading this, and I look forward to hearing from you." (drop the thanks if needed to keep one page).
- Under option: "I'm aware there's a <notice/restriction> on your title for an agreement dated <date> with <company>. ... Agreements like that are often for a fixed number of years, and after this long it may have run its course, be coming to an end, or simply have gone quiet. ... I'm not asking you to do anything that cuts across an agreement that is still in force."
- Part of a holding: "To be clear, it's only this one field I'm writing about, not the rest of your land at <name>."

## Rejected wording
"your field is the kind of land we're looking for"; "yours is one we'd like to take on"; "take your land forward ourselves" (first letter; acceptable only in the under option variant); "we hope to settle on a site before the end of the year"; "I know a letter like this raises questions... in my experience it's a good deal simpler"; "your field sits in an area the council has identified for new homes and land like yours is in demand"; "I also know..."; anything telling the owner what the council thinks or what the land is worth.

## Every point of BuildUp's template, paraphrased, never copied, not in its order
Excuse the approach; who we are; we have identified their land and are interested; active need in the area and their land meets our criteria; questions and hesitations; simpler than people think, owners surprised; get in touch to have the facts and decide now or later; informal, no obligation; phone and email; thanks.

## Facts
- Every statement about a title must come from a document about that title; never infer a promotion or an assessment from position or from a neighbouring plot.
- A register notice proves an agreement existed, not that it is live; the letter is how to find out. Do not hold a letter on an assumption.
- Co-owners at one address: one joint letter. An owner elsewhere: their own letter, saying the others have been written to.
- Full first names where the register gives them, initials where it does not; "Dear Mr <surname>"; the address block matches the envelope.

## Format and filing
- Letterhead; signature between "Yours sincerely," and the printed name, with the role beneath and no company name.
- "Our ref" is the profile's prefix plus the title number (the main title for a multi-title site), on the letter and the map.
- File name "<title number> - <owner's full name>.pdf"; the address in brackets only when one owner gets two letters.
- A batch is delivered complete, in one go. A correction is delivered as the whole letter again.
- Each letter goes with the colour map in a hand addressed envelope.

## Emails to agents and promoters
Joint venture only: we fund and run planning at our own cost and risk; they share in the value on sale. Never an upfront payment. No figures or percentages until the programme has reviewed the appraisal. Never mention other sites nearby. Never pre-empt objections they have not raised. Ask for a call "this week". To a named person where known. Do not echo their own words back ("thanks for reading the page properly" reads as patronising).

## Anything written to a mentor or programme (for example BuildUp)
First person singular, "I". Complete and final; nothing hidden.
