# Call to an owner with a long planning history (several refusals or withdrawals)

Example: a closed pub with a protected green and several planning attempts over ten years. Adapt the bracketed parts.

## 1. Open
"Hi <name>, it's {short_name} from {company}. You got in touch through our website about <property> on <road>. Is now a good time to talk for ten minutes or so?"
*Wait for yes.*
"I've had a look at the property and the planning history, and I'd like to understand it from your side before I say anything, if that's alright."

## 2. Their story
"How did you come to own it?"
*Silence; only "right", "I see".*
"And when did <the business> stop trading?"

## 3. Today
"What's happening with the building at the moment? Is anyone in it, or is it standing empty?"
*Only if they do not mention the latest permission:*
"I saw the <shop> permission came through <last summer>. Is that something you're going ahead with, or was that more about keeping options open?"
*The word "options" lets them say it was a fallback.*

## 4. The planning history, their version
"You've obviously had a few goes at the planning over the years. Talk me through what happened with those, what worked and what didn't."
*If the key scheme is not mentioned:*
"And the <flats> scheme. Why did that get pulled?"
*This is the key answer.*

## 5. What they want
"If you could wave a wand, what would you like to happen with the place? Sell it as it is, sell it with a permission, or keep it and have someone develop it with you?"
"And is there a timeframe on your side, anything driving the timing?"
*Mortgage, divorce, retirement, lender, tax year.*

## 6. The whole site
"Has anyone ever looked at <the building, the car park and the green> together, as one site, rather than just <the green> on its own?"
*If asked what you mean:*
"<The green> has been refused three times and it's still protected open space, so I'd be cautious about going at that again on its own. <The building and the forecourt> might be a different conversation. That's what I'd want our planning team to look at."
*No numbers.*

## 7. Ownership and paperwork
"Is the property in your name alone, or are there other family members or a lender involved?"
"Do you have the drawings and the council correspondence from the previous applications? Those would save our planners a lot of time."

## 8. Close
"That's really helpful. What I'll do is take this to our planning team. They'll look at <the whole site> as one, and I'll come back to you within three weeks with what we think is realistic and how we'd structure it. If you can send over whatever paperwork you have in the meantime, my email is {email}. Is there a best time of day to reach you?"
*Stop. Let them end the call.*

*After the call: the answers on why the key scheme was withdrawn and whether the latest permission is being used decide whether the appraisal is a conversion scheme or something smaller.*
