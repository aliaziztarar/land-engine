# First call to an owner who enquired through the website

Example: an owner who bought a field at auction with a 25 percent uplift clause and then enquired through the website. Adapt the bracketed parts. Based on BuildUp's "Initial landowner discussion feedback".

## 1. Opening
"Hello, is that <name>? It's {short_name} from {company}. You sent an enquiry through our website <last night> about the field at <place>. Is now a good time for five or ten minutes?"
*If no, fix a time.*
"Thanks for getting in touch. I've had a proper look at the land this morning, and I'll share what I found in a minute. But I'd rather hear it from you first. What's the story with the field, what drew you to it?"
*Let them talk. Do not interrupt. Make notes.*

## 2. Ownership
"And it's all gone through now, you've completed? Is it in your own name, or a company?"
"Anyone else involved, a partner, family, or a lender?"

## 3. Any clause or agreement (only if there is one)
"I saw it came through <agent> at the <month> auction. The particulars mentioned a <25 per cent uplift clause running for 40 years>. Do you know how that's worded, what actually triggers it?"
*Whatever they say:*
"Okay, that's common, we'll factor it in. Would you be happy to send me the legal pack? It'll save us both guessing."

## 4. Their plans
"What's your thinking for it? Sell it on with planning, or build yourself?"
"What would a good outcome look like for you, say two years from now?"

## 5. Who else
"Have you spoken to anyone yet about it, a planning consultant, the council, the parish, anyone else like us?"

## 6. The land
"Just so I've got it right: you own right up to <the lane>, nothing in between?"
*Ransom strip question: does the title reach the public road so an entrance can be built without crossing anyone else's land.*
"Anything in the legal pack I should know about, rights of way, covenants, services?"

## 7. Timing
"Are you working to any timescale, or is this exploratory for now?"

## 8. Now tell them
*Who we are: {role}, the company, and the planning team you work with (planners, architects, highways, ecology), honestly described. Desk review: real potential in principle; name the helpful facts at the level of "sits against the settlement boundary", "the council's housing position is helpful"; name design points as design, not blockers.*
*Describe the deal in the words of the user's business model (profile/profile.md). For an option or conditional contract model, for example:*
"We agree a fixed price with you up front, in writing. We then pay for all of the planning, surveys, design, council fees, and any appeal. You never contribute. If we get permission, we buy at the agreed price. If we don't, the agreement ends and you keep the land, owing nothing. We also contribute to your own solicitor's fees so you get independent advice."

## 9. If asked for a figure
"I never put a number on a first call, it wouldn't be fair to either of us. The next step is for me to go through it properly with our planning team and come back to you with what's realistically possible and a ballpark figure. Would you be open to that?"

## 10. Close
"So, next steps. You send me the legal pack. I'll go through the site with the team and come back to you within the week with our view and a figure to talk around. If it stacks up for both of us, we meet, ideally on the land. Does that work?"
"What's the best way to reach you, this number or email?"
"Thanks <name>. Speak to you within the week."
