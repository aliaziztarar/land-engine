# Email to a land agent acting for the owner (invented example)

Subject: Land off Hill Road, Ashcombe (titles <TITLE> and <TITLE>)

Dear <name or Sir or Madam>,

I am writing about the land on the corner of Hill Road and Mill Lane, Ashcombe, held under titles <TITLE> and <TITLE>. The site appeared in <council>'s land availability assessment as site <ref>, where the council found it suitable, available and achievable, and your firm is named as the agent acting for the owner.

We are a strategic land business. We work with landowners to take their land through planning at our own cost and our own risk, and the owner is paid when the land is sold with planning permission. If permission is not granted, the owner pays nothing and keeps the land.

I would like to know whether your client would be open to a conversation on that basis. It would also help to understand where the site stands at the moment, in particular whether it was put forward in the council's recent call for sites, and whether any other party is already involved.

Would you have time for a short call this week?

Kind regards,

{name}
{company}
{mobile}

# Rules for these emails
- Never an upfront payment; always a joint venture where the other party shares in the value at the end.
- No figures and no percentages until the programme has reviewed the appraisal.
- Never mention other sites being pursued nearby.
- Do not pre-empt objections they have not raised.
- Ask for a call this week.
- Send from the business email address in the profile, to a named person where one is known.
