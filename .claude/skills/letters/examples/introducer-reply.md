# Replies to introducers and land sourcers

Checks before relying on any sourcer: introducing owners to buyers for a fee is estate agency work (Estate Agents Act 1979), so ask which property redress scheme they belong to (The Property Ombudsman or the Property Redress Scheme) and whether they are registered with HMRC for anti money laundering supervision; agree written fee terms before any site name or address is exchanged; check the owner knows the site is being offered and has agreed; never share the user's sites or owners' names until terms are signed. Many introducers send one site to several buyers, which turns it into an auction; ask for exclusivity (a registered site not offered elsewhere for, say, 30 days, extended while terms are agreed) in return for protecting their introduction.

What to tell them the user wants: the criteria in `profile/profile.md` (typical: freehold land of about two acres or more, at the edge of or well related to a town or village, safe access, mostly outside the high flood zone, no strong designation across the site, councils short of housing land or with an out of date plan, every owner willing to talk and not tied into an agreement with time left to run).

Registration: they email the title number or a plan, a note of contact so far and the owner's consent; the user checks their records and confirms in writing within two working days whether the site is new to them; a registered site is protected for an agreed period; the user does not approach a registered owner except through the introducer.

Fees: from `profile/profile.md` (the user's own terms). Common shapes: a small sum on signing with the owner, credited against the total, and the balance when the sale completes after planning; a percentage of the agreed land price with a cap (so the introducer has no reason to push the owner's price up); nothing beyond the first sum if planning fails. Leave amounts out of the first email; promise one page of written terms.

Opening that works: "Thanks for your email. Yes, we do work with independent introducers, and the area you cover is one we're keen on." Do not echo their own words back.
