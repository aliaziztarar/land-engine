# Declining a site the user cannot take (owner enquiry)

Principle: an owner who is turned down should come away thinking the user was honest, with a clear picture of how the land was assessed, some free practical help, and the door left open.

Structure (example: a Green Belt farmstead with listed buildings and an old conversion permission):
1. Thanks and acknowledgement.
2. Confirm which property was looked at.
3. Correct any earlier estimate (for example a number of homes guessed from the acreage alone).
4. The hard news in plain words (the designation; that size does not change it; what the council's own reviews and assessments said).
5. The real opportunity if there is one (for example converting the existing buildings; earlier approvals show what officers accepted).
6. What to expect if they go it alone (listed building consent, shared access rights, surveys, any local habitats payment, whether the old permission is still alive).
7. Why the user cannot take it (they pay for all the planning and agree a price at the start; on a small conversion any price they could offer would undervalue the property).
8. Free help: introductions to buyers or to a trusted architect, at no charge and with no obligation.
9. The door left open.

Rules: nothing about the user's programme, profit shares or what happens after planning; introductions stay free (charging for introducing buyers is estate agency work, with its own legal duties); check with the programme before introducing anyone to its professionals; write in the user's own plain voice; site note: "Declined politely, offered introductions. Follow up only if they reply."
